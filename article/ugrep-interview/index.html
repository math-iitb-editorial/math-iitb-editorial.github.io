Sitting Down with the Undergraduate Representative

Interviewee Vishnu Ravi
Published: 2026-04-04

Motivation to Become UG Representative

My main motivation for running for this position was that I observed a lack of understanding among the student body about the role of the UG Representative. I realised that many important decisions and processes happened behind the scenes, and I wanted to be in a position to communicate student concerns to the faculty effectively. I also tend to be outspoken, which helped me feel confident in representing student issues.

Additionally, I had already made an effort to interact with juniors, batchmates, and seniors. Through these interactions, I became more aware of student concerns, which naturally led me to consider the UG Representative role. It also gave me a structured way to stay connected with students across batches.

However, after taking on the role, I realized that institutional change is slow and often difficult. Despite that, a new motivation emerged: giving back to the institute. I have gained a lot from this place, and I wanted to contribute in return. The role became more about service than impact or recognition.

Perception of the Position

Before applying, I believed the role allowed for meaningful change and visibility. I also admit that I was partially motivated by recognition. However, after taking on the role, I realised that most work is incremental and often goes unnoticed. My perspective shifted from seeking recognition to focusing on service.

Additionally, in my second year, I had a very dismissive view of positions of responsibility. I thought they were unproductive or popularity-driven. That perception changed completely after I understood how much of the institute's functioning depends on these roles. Despite this though I feel most students are unaware of the work happening behind the scenes. They often assume the role has more authority than it actually does. When issues remain unresolved, they may believe the UG Representative is ineffective, whereas the reality is that many decisions are beyond the role's control. So there is a dichotomy between the way that these PoRs are integral to the running of the institute but also the limitations of the position.

Contributions and Responsibilities

My responsibilities included both visible and behind-the-scenes work. Some of the key contributions were:

  • Raising concerns about limited elective options.
  • Participating in DUGC meetings and proposing increased flexibility in electives.
  • Handling retagging and graduation-related administrative issues.
  • Addressing unexpected academic and administrative issues such as course availability, portal errors, and policy inconsistencies.
  • Working on proposals related to degree flexibility and independent study options.

There are also many overlaps with other positions such as DGSec as well as DAMPC but the UG Representative focuses on academic representation and policy-level issues. The DGSec primarily handles departmental events and student activities. DAMPC focuses on mentorship, student welfare, and individual support systems. While there is some overlap, the roles are largely distinct in function.

Creating Balance

A significant portion of my work involved resolving immediate student issues, especially during registration periods, where I prioritized being available full-time. When faced with both immediate issues and long-term policy changes, immediate issues always took priority because they directly impacted students in real time. Long-term proposals required consistent effort over time. During registration periods, I focused entirely on resolving issues. Outside those periods, I dedicated more time to policy work.

Policy Change and Power

The main challenge to policy change was institutional resistance. For example, increasing flexibility in electives required balancing credit constraints, departmental expectations, and feasibility. Even when proposals were reasonable, they often faced opposition due to concerns about academic rigour or resource limitations.

So my main responsibility boiled down to representing student concerns within the framework of existing rules. If an issue aligns with official regulations, I can ensure it is addressed. In gray areas, I can advocate for students. However, strict rules cannot be overridden by the UG Representative.

Message to the Department and Future UG Representative

From my tenure as UG Representative, I have realised there needs to be greater respect for student representatives. Ignoring communication or dismissing concerns undermines student voices. Increased transparency and collaboration between faculty and student representatives would significantly improve outcomes.

And to the future UG Representatives, your focus should be on service rather than outcomes. To continue to push for student issues even when progress is slow. Maintain composure and avoid taking institutional resistance personally, build strong communication with class representatives to encourage collaboration across student groups.

Final Note

This role showed me how dedicated students are, both with and without formal positions. Many contribute significantly without recognition. It also changed my perspective on responsibility and service. Being part of such a community has been a deeply enriching experience.
