In Conversation with the Department General Secretary

Interviewee: Ujjwal Kumar Mishra
Published: 2026-04-07

Introduction

Hi, my name is Ujjwal, and I'm a second-year M.Sc. Maths student in the department. I am the General Secretary of the Mathematics Department, and my responsibilities include handling student issues and organising events for department members. There are two sides to the job: the Mathematics Association side and the department-level academic side. The job of a department general secretary is to oversee them both.

Perception of the Role

There was no prior perception, as I was unaware that such a position existed during my first eight months on campus. Then, in my position as Class Representative, I became more aware of the college administration, and through this experience and my previous experience at my previous college, I thought I could give this position a chance. I did not at any point think that I was not fit for the position. I knew I wanted to do it, and I had prior experience, so I knew I could do it again.

From the outside, though, I think students believe the role is predominantly about organising events. They do not see the other aspects. The position involves constant push for policy changes that, on average, can take several years to implement in their entirety. People have the misconception that change happens quickly and expect quick resolutions, which is not practical.

Issues Identified in the Department

The primary issue we saw was UG–PG interaction, and to a lesser extent, PG–PhD interaction. We wanted to conduct events where everyone could come together and interact. This would increase communication and, from an administrative perspective, holding a united front with open communication would strengthen the push for change. Even if PGAC and UGAC find common ground on their issues, the numbers will help draw attention to the problem, and proposals can be made stronger, with a higher chance of acceptance.

Another thing we identified, based on student feedback, was that the course curriculum was outdated for mathematics and statistics students. Many students come to this program seeking more industry-oriented experience, but because the department offers limited elective options, it is much more research-oriented, leaving many unprepared for industry. There should be greater flexibility and opportunities for thesis work, as there is currently no such facility for master's students.

Another issue concerns summer courses and their heavy reliance on professor availability. This may lead to many having to take extensions. There should be a more formal method for guaranteeing summer courses and giving students a chance to clear courses within the time of their degree.

Initiatives and Progress

Many initiatives were started, and I believe this is one of the most important parts of the process. Even if not completed, identifying the issue and bringing it to the administration's attention is a step in the right direction. Most issues that appear are systemic issues and so take a long time to fix.

One of the main things we pushed for was PG TA-ships, as there is no official way within this department for PGs to gain this experience. Currently, they cannot be paid, but they can take it up informally, and PGAC will provide them with a certificate as proof of their experience. A lot more work needs to be done in the area, and it is for the next DGSec to take this up and push it forward.

Intersection of UG Representative and DGSec

As Department General Secretary, it is my belief that everything in the department falls under my responsibility, and I can delegate tasks to others whom I believe understand and can handle them. The UG Representative is someone I delegate UG issues to. If a UG issue comes to my attention, I inform the UG Representative, and any changes they wish to push in the DUGC would be reviewed to stay aware of happenings in the department.

Council Structure

As DGSec, I was also the student head of the Mathematics Association and responsible for organising departmental events. The main issue we faced in running events was funding. The primary source of funding is the IITB Mathematics Olympiad and Association Fees. With more funding, it will be easier to run better events.

Another thing I wanted to push was alumni connection, which until now has been almost non-existent. This is something the next DGSec is passionate about and will take up further. There are also many positions that work in collaboration with the association but fall under different institute bodies. These positions should work more collaboratively as, in the end, the purpose is the same: to serve the people in the department. On a similar note, even positions within this department, such as Class Representative, need a more formal selection process for clarity and accountability. I strongly believe that if you are a part of a council, your work is not limited to your position title. During events, everyone is expected to participate and be present to deal with events at hand. Teams require flexibility and coordination.

Advice for Future DGSec and Students

The main advice I would like to give is that everyone has their own path, and that, though many people will give their input, whether solicited or not, you need to stay rational and make your own decisions. It is important to make your own mistakes and learn from them. This is the last place where you will be able to learn freely, so do not be afraid to speak up, participate and make mistakes.
